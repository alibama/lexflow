For a licence regulated by the Business and Professions Code, after a board serves a notice of denial under § 485(b). Not for licences under the State Bar Act (Chapter 4 of Division 3), the Alcoholic Beverage Control Act (Division 9) or the Gambling Control Act (Chapter 5 of Division 8): § 476(a) says Division 1.5, which holds §§ 480 to 487, does not apply to them, so this draft does not apply to them either. Section 480(i) leaves the licensing authority of the State Athletic Commission, the Bureau for Private Postsecondary Education and the California Horse Racing Board unchanged. Some practice acts and divisions have conviction rules of their own. The Board of Pharmacy may deny “Notwithstanding Section 480” (§ 4202.6). The Board of Accountancy has grounds “In addition to” those of § 480, with no seven-year limit for some financial felonies (§ 5100.2). Three sections direct a board to deny a licence to a person subject to sex-offender registration under Penal Code § 290, with exceptions (§§ 1687, 1958.1, 2660.5). Section 4989.24, in the Educational Psychologist Practice Act, bars a licence for a conviction, in California, in another state or in a territory of the United States, of a crime involving sexual abuse of children, or for a person subject to that registration or the equivalent in another state or territory, and its subdivision (b) applies the conditions for denial in § 480 to such a denial. For a state cannabis licence under Division 10, § 26057(b)(2) lets the Department of Cannabis Control deny for conduct that is a ground for denial under Chapter 2 of Division 1.5 (commencing with § 480), “except as otherwise specified” in § 26057 and § 26059. Read the practice act or division that governs your licence. This draft is not for licences under other codes.

Notes for you (keep these; they are not part of the letter)

Ask for a hearing, in writing

Required by the statute

Send the request in writing within 60 days after service of the notice of denial. Without a timely written request the right to a hearing is waived.

“Unless written request for hearing is made within the 60-day period, the applicant’s right to a hearing is deemed waived.”

Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 485(b)

Proof of the dismissal

Required by the statute

Only for a dismissal under Penal Code § 1203.4, 1203.4a, 1203.41 or 1203.42 that is not on the Department of Justice report.

The statute does not say what counts as proof.

“An applicant who has a conviction that has been dismissed pursuant to Section 1203.4, 1203.4a, 1203.41, or 1203.42 of the Penal Code shall provide proof of the dismissal if it is not reflected on the report furnished by the Department of Justice.”

Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 480(c)

Licences outside this draft

Good to know

Division 1.5 of the Business and Professions Code, which holds §§ 480 to 487, does not apply to licences under the State Bar Act (Chapter 4 of Division 3), the Alcoholic Beverage Control Act (Division 9) or the Gambling Control Act (Chapter 5 of Division 8). This draft does not apply to those licences. Under subdivision (b), § 494.5 still applies to them.

“Except as provided in subdivision (b), nothing in this division shall apply to the licensure or registration of persons pursuant to Chapter 4 (commencing with Section 6000) of Division 3, or pursuant to Division 9 (commencing with Section 23000) or pursuant to Chapter 5 (commencing with Section 19800) of Division 8.”

Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 476(a)

Three bodies keep their own rules

Good to know

Section 480 leaves the licensing authority of these three bodies unchanged.

“This section does not in any way modify or otherwise affect the existing authority of the following entities in regard to licensure: (1) The State Athletic Commission. (2) The Bureau for Private Postsecondary Education. (3) The California Horse Racing Board.”

Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 480(i)

If the board served a statement of issues instead

Good to know

This draft is for a notice of denial under § 485(b). If the board instead filed and served a statement of issues under § 485(a), the Government Code procedure for that applies, and this letter may not be the right step.

“File and serve a statement of issues in accordance with Chapter 5 (commencing with Section 11500) of Part 1 of Division 3 of Title 2 of the Government Code.”

Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 485(a)

When a board may deny for a conviction

Good to know

Section 480(a) lists two conditions. (1) A substantially related conviction within the seven years before the application, or a substantially related conviction for which the applicant is incarcerated now or was released from incarceration within those seven years; subparagraphs (A) and (B) lift the seven-year limit for some crimes. (2) Formal discipline by a licensing board in or outside California within those seven years, for substantially related professional misconduct that would have been cause for discipline before the board. Paragraph (2) has two qualifiers. Discipline within the seven years is not a basis for denial if it was based on a conviction dismissed under Penal Code § 1203.4, 1203.4a, 1203.41, 1203.42 or 1203.425, or a comparable dismissal or expungement. Discipline older than seven years can be a ground only if it was for conduct that, by a California physician and surgeon, would have been sexual abuse, misconduct or relations with a patient under § 726, or sexual exploitation under § 729(a). Some practice acts have rules of their own (see the scope above).

“a board may deny a license regulated by this code on the grounds that the applicant has been convicted of a crime or has been subject to formal discipline only if either of the following conditions are met”

Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 480(a)

Exceptions to the seven-year limit

Good to know

The seven-year limit does not apply to (A) a serious felony under Penal Code § 1192.7 or a crime that carries sex-offender registration under Penal Code § 290(d)(2) or (3), or (B) for the licences listed in (i) to (v), a felony financial crime directly and adversely related to the licence's fiduciary qualifications, functions or duties, under the board's regulations.

“However, the preceding seven-year limitation shall not apply in either of the following situations: (A) The applicant was convicted of a serious felony, as defined in Section 1192.7 of the Penal Code or a crime for which registration is required pursuant to paragraph (2) or (3) of subdivision (d) of Section 290 of the Penal Code. (B) The applicant was convicted of a financial crime currently classified as a felony that is directly and adversely related to the fiduciary qualifications, functions, or duties of the business or profession for which the application is made, pursuant to regulations adopted by the board, and for which the applicant is seeking licensure under any of the following: (i) Chapter 6 (commencing with Section 6500) of Division 3. (ii) Chapter 9 (commencing with Section 7000) of Division 3. (iii) Chapter 11.3 (commencing with Section 7512) of Division 3. (iv) Licensure as a funeral director or cemetery manager under Chapter 12 (commencing with Section 7600) of Division 3. (v) Division 4 (commencing with Section 10000).”

Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 480(a)(1)

Formal discipline as a ground for denial

Good to know

The second condition of § 480(a), quoted in full with both qualifiers: discipline based on a dismissed conviction is not a basis for denial, and discipline older than seven years counts only for the conduct named in the last sentence.

“The applicant has been subjected to formal discipline by a licensing board in or outside California within the preceding seven years from the date of application based on professional misconduct that would have been cause for discipline before the board for which the present application is made and that is substantially related to the qualifications, functions, or duties of the business or profession for which the present application is made. However, prior disciplinary action by a licensing board within the preceding seven years shall not be the basis for denial of a license if the basis for that disciplinary action was a conviction that has been dismissed pursuant to Section 1203.4, 1203.4a, 1203.41, 1203.42, or 1203.425 of the Penal Code or a comparable dismissal or expungement. Formal discipline that occurred earlier than seven years preceding the date of application may be grounds for denial of a license only if the formal discipline was for conduct that, if committed in this state by a physician and surgeon licensed pursuant to Chapter 5 (commencing with Section 2000) of Division 2, would have constituted an act of sexual abuse, misconduct, or relations with a patient pursuant to Section 726 or sexual exploitation as defined in subdivision (a) of Section 729.”

Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 480(a)(2)

What the board's criteria for a substantially related crime include

Good to know

The board's criteria for whether a crime is substantially related to the licence include the nature and gravity of the offense, the years since the offense, and the nature and duties of the profession.

“Criteria for determining whether a crime is substantially related to the qualifications, functions, or duties of the business or profession a board regulates shall include all of the following: (1) The nature and gravity of the offense. (2) The number of years elapsed since the date of the offense. (3) The nature and duties of the profession in which the applicant seeks licensure or in which the licensee is licensed.”

Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 481(b)

What the board shall put in the notice of denial

What the other side has to do

Read the notice for the board's own challenge or reconsideration procedure.

“If a board decides to deny an application for licensure based solely or in part on the applicant’s conviction history, the board shall notify the applicant in writing of all of the following: (A) The denial or disqualification of licensure. (B) Any existing procedure the board has for the applicant to challenge the decision or to request reconsideration. (C) That the applicant has the right to appeal the board’s decision.”

Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 480(f)(3)

A copy of your own conviction history

Good to know

Section 480(f)(3)(D) puts this in the notice of denial too. The Department of Justice's form for requesting a copy of your own record is BCIA 8016RR.

“The processes for the applicant to request a copy of the applicant’s complete conviction history and question the accuracy or completeness of the record pursuant to Sections 11122 to 11127 of the Penal Code.”

Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 480(f)(3)(D)

When the board holds the hearing

What the other side has to do

The 90 days run from the date of your request, unless you ask for or agree in writing to a postponement or continuance. The Office of Administrative Hearings may add up to 45 days (up to 180 in cases of alleged examination or licensing fraud), with no more than two such orders or grants.

“If a hearing is requested by the applicant, the board shall conduct such hearing within 90 days from the date the hearing is requested unless the applicant shall request or agree in writing to a postponement or continuance of the hearing. Notwithstanding the above, the Office of Administrative Hearings may order, or on a showing of good cause, grant a request for, up to 45 additional days within which to conduct a hearing, except in cases involving alleged examination or licensing fraud, in which cases the period may be up to 180 days. In no case shall more than two such orders be made or requests be granted.”

Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 487

Reapplying

Good to know

The decision or notice states the earliest date you may reapply.

“The earliest date on which the applicant may reapply for a license which shall be one year from the effective date of the decision, or service of the notice under subdivision (b) of Section 485, unless the board prescribes an earlier date or a later date is prescribed by another statute.”

Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 486(a)

Evidence when you reapply

Good to know

The decision or notice of denial also tells you that all competent evidence of rehabilitation presented will be considered if you reapply.

“That all competent evidence of rehabilitation presented will be considered upon a reapplication.”

Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 486(b)

The board serves its rehabilitation criteria with the notice

What the other side has to do

With the decision or the notice of denial, the board serves a copy of its criteria for evaluating rehabilitation (§ 482).

“Along with the decision, or the notice under subdivision (b) of Section 485, the board shall serve a copy of the criteria relating to rehabilitation formulated under Section 482.”

Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 486
