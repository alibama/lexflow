Notes for you (keep these; they are not part of the letter)

The records you are asking for

Required by the statute

Describe the records as specifically as you reasonably can, for example by subject, date range, office or type of document.

“A request for public records shall identify the requested records with reasonable specificity.”

Va. Code § 2.2-3704(B)

Your name and legal address

Only if they ask for it

The custodian may require your name and legal address. You can give them now or wait to be asked.

“The custodian may require the requester to provide his name and legal address.”

Va. Code § 2.2-3704(A)

More time

Good to know

If the public body answers within five working days that it is not practically possible to respond in time, it gets seven more work days, or 60 work days for a request for criminal investigative files under § 2.2-3706.1, to give one of the other responses.

“If the response is made within five working days, the public body shall have an additional seven work days or, in the case of a request for criminal investigative files pursuant to § 2.2-3706.1 , 60 work days in which to provide one of the four preceding responses.”

Va. Code § 2.2-3704(B)(4)

A court can give more time

Good to know

For an extraordinary volume of records or an extraordinarily lengthy search, the public body may ask a court for more time. Before it does, it makes reasonable efforts to agree with you on how the records are produced.

“Any public body may petition the appropriate court for additional time to respond to a request for records when the request is for an extraordinary volume of records or requires an extraordinarily lengthy search, and a response by the public body within the time required by this chapter will prevent the public body from meeting its operational responsibilities. Before proceeding with the petition, however, the public body shall make reasonable efforts to reach an agreement with the requester concerning the production of the records requested.”

Va. Code § 2.2-3704(C)

No answer

Good to know

Silence counts as a denial.

“Failure to respond to a request for records shall be deemed a denial of the request and shall constitute a violation of this chapter.”

Va. Code § 2.2-3704(E)

Charges

Good to know

Charges are capped at the public body's actual cost.

“a public body may make reasonable charges not to exceed its actual cost incurred in accessing, duplicating, supplying, or searching for the requested records”

Va. Code § 2.2-3704(F)

Notice of charges before a search

What the other side has to do

Before it searches, the public body tells you in writing that it may charge its actual cost, and asks whether you want a cost estimate first.

“Prior to conducting a search for records, the public body shall notify the requester in writing that the public body may make reasonable charges not to exceed its actual cost incurred in accessing, duplicating, supplying, or searching for requested records and inquire of the requester whether he would like to request a cost estimate in advance of the supplying of the requested records.”

Va. Code § 2.2-3704(F)

The clock pauses for the estimate

Good to know

The response time stops between the estimate and your answer to it.

“The period within which the public body shall respond under this section shall be tolled for the amount of time that elapses between notice of the cost estimate and the response of the requester.”

Va. Code § 2.2-3704(F)

Answer the estimate within 30 days

Good to know

If you do not answer a cost estimate within 30 days, the request is treated as withdrawn.

“If the public body receives no response from the requester within 30 days of sending the cost estimate, the request shall be deemed to be withdrawn.”

Va. Code § 2.2-3704(F)

Deposit when charges are likely to exceed $200

Good to know

If the public body determines in advance that charges are likely to exceed $200, it may ask for a deposit, up to the amount of that advance determination, before it continues to process the request. The deposit counts toward the final cost, and the response time pauses from notice of the advance determination until you answer.

“In any case where a public body determines in advance that charges for producing the requested records are likely to exceed $200, the public body may, before continuing to process the request, require the requester to pay a deposit not to exceed the amount of the advance determination. The deposit shall be credited toward the final cost of supplying the requested records. The period within which the public body shall respond under this section shall be tolled for the amount of time that elapses between notice of the advance determination and the response of the requester.”

Va. Code § 2.2-3704(H)

Earlier unpaid bills

Good to know

Old unpaid charges for earlier requests can hold up a new one.

“Before processing a request for records, a public body may require the requester to pay any amounts owed to the public body for previous requests for records that remain unpaid 30 days or more after billing.”

Va. Code § 2.2-3704(I)

Existing records only

Good to know

Ask for records that exist. A summary or abstract is a matter of agreement with the public body.

“no public body shall be required to create a new record if the record does not already exist. However, a public body may abstract or summarize information under such terms and conditions as agreed between the requester and the public body.”

Va. Code § 2.2-3704(D)
