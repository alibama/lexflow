## How do I request public records in City of Charlottesville?

**Confidence 1.00** (state law 1.00, local 1.00, contact 1.00; state model checked by Claude).

Charlottesville handles public records requests under the Virginia Freedom of Information Act (Va. Code § 2.2-3700 et seq.). The easiest way to submit is the city's online Records Request portal (GovQA). You can also mail a request to the City Attorney's Office at P.O. Box 911, Charlottesville, VA 22902, fax it to 434-970-3890, or deliver it in person at the City Attorney's Office. The city's Requesting Records page says email and phone requests are also accepted. The city must respond within five working days, or within seven more working days if it explains in writing why the deadline is practically impossible. You can ask for a cost estimate in advance, and requests that take 15 minutes or less to fulfil are free. The city's pages describe no internal appeal. If you are denied, you can petition the general district or circuit court to compel compliance, or ask the Virginia FOIA Council for a nonbinding advisory opinion.

### What state law requires (Va. Code Ann. § 2.2-3704)

1. **Request records with reasonable specificity** - Requester (citizen of the Commonwealth, or media circulating or broadcasting in Virginia) (obligation). § 2.2-3704(B)
   > A request for public records shall identify the requested records with reasonable specificity.
2. **Notify of charges; offer cost estimate** - Public body (records custodian) (obligation). § 2.2-3704(F)
   > Prior to conducting a search for records, the public body shall notify the requester in writing that the public body may make reasonable charges
3. **Try to agree on production with requester** - Public body (records custodian) (obligation). § 2.2-3704(C)
   > Before proceeding with the petition, however, the public body shall make reasonable efforts to reach an agreement with the requester
4. **Petition court for more time** - Public body (records custodian) (permission). § 2.2-3704(C)
   > Any public body may petition the appropriate court for additional time to respond to a request for records when the request is for an extraordinary volume of records or requires an extraordinarily lengthy search
5. **Provide records or answer in writing** - Public body (records custodian), within 5 business days (obligation). § 2.2-3704(B)
   > provide the requested records to the requester or make one of the following responses in writing
6. **Supply inspection or copies at lowest cost** - Public body (records custodian) (obligation). § 2.2-3704(F)
   > shall make all reasonable efforts to supply the requested records at the lowest possible cost
7. **Provide records or answer within extension** - Public body (records custodian), within 7 business days (obligation). § 2.2-3704(B)(4)
   > in which to provide one of the four preceding responses

### What City of Charlottesville adds

2. **Submit the request online, by mail, by fax, or in person** - Requester to the City Attorney's Office / FOIA & Records Officer. 4. **Wait for the city's response (5 working days, extendable by 7 more)** - City of Charlottesville. Within five working days the city must do one of four things: provide the records, withhold them in writing citing the Code of Virginia section, release them with the exempt parts redacted, or tell you in writing that the records cannot be found or do not exist. ### Caveats and gaps

- Under Va. Code § 2.2-3704(A), the right of access belongs to citizens of the Commonwealth and to qualifying Virginia media. Out-of-state requesters may be refused. The earlier claim about the city's past MuckRock responses was not re-verified.
- The FOIA Officers directory lists several officers: the FOIA & Records Officer (434-970-3827) and the FOIA and Public Records Officer, plus separate officers for the Sheriff, Treasurer, Commissioner of the Revenue, and Airport Authority. Confirm the current contact on the directory before you write.
- The Requesting Records page says requests may be made by email or by phone. The Submit a FOIA Request page lists no email address. FOIA@charlottesville.gov comes only from the 'Email Us' link on the staff directory.
- Not re-verified: Police Department records are said to be handled under Police General Order 523.20 through the department's Public Information Officer. Ask the department directly.
- University of Virginia records are not city records. UVA runs its own FOIA process (not verified here).
- Pages retrieved 2026-10-09. Check them for updates.
- Subsection E: failure to respond "shall be deemed a denial of the request and shall constitute a violation of this chapter." Both response deadlines route their timers (consequence 'deemed-denial') to the end state deemed_denied, kept separate from a written withholding or not-found response (no_records_released). For the five-working-day deadline this follows E directly. For the B.4 extension it is an inference: a written B.4 response has already been given when that timer runs, and deemed denial is read from E together with B.4's requirement to provide "one of the four preceding responses" within the extension. Enforcement by petition for mandamus or injunction under Va. Code § 2.2-3713 is not modelled.
- Subsection A opens records to citizens of the Commonwealth and to representatives of newspapers and magazines circulating, and radio and television stations broadcasting, in or into Virginia; this section grants no right to anyone else, and whether a body answers other requesters is not stated here.
- This is the Virginia process, so it governs Charlottesville and other Virginia localities only; requests to the District of Columbia (D.C. Code § 2-532) or to Baltimore (Md. Code, General Provisions § 4-203) follow their own statutes and are modelled separately.
- Optional steps that would rejoin the main path are not modelled as separate steps: the custodian may require the requester's name and legal address (A); the cost-estimate exchange tolls the clock, and the request is deemed withdrawn if the requester does not answer an estimate within 30 days (F); a deposit may be required when charges are likely to exceed $200 (H); and unpaid bills 30 days or more past due may have to be paid first (I).
- Court petition (C): the condition extraordinary_burden_and_intends_petition stands for both statutory prongs (an extraordinary volume of records or an extraordinarily lengthy search, and a timely response would prevent the body from meeting its operational responsibilities) plus the body's choice to petition, since the agreement duty applies only before proceeding with the petition. The path is an exclusive alternative to the ordinary response and ends at filing; the statute does not say what follows an agreement or the court's ruling.
- Extension (B.4): the extra time is available only if the B.4 response is made within five working days, which the model reflects by reaching respond_extended only from a timely respond. The text says 'an additional seven work days' without saying whether they run from the end of the five-day period or from the B.4 response; the timer is a boundary event on respond_extended, so it runs from the B.4 response, which can be shorter than seven work days counted from the end of the five-day period. Criminal investigative files (§ 2.2-3706.1) get 60 work days instead; the timer shows only the seven-day figure.
- Subsection F's opening exception for scholastic records under § 2.2-3705.4 A 1 qualifies the permission to make charges; the text does not say whether the later duty to notify the requester of charges before a search still applies to such requests, so notify_charges is shown without a condition.
- No new record need be created (D), electronic records must be produced in a medium the body regularly uses (G), and a body that transferred records for storage remains their custodian (J); these shape the response but are not separate steps.
- The first response and the B.4 extended response rejoin as exclusive merges: supply and no_records_released are each modelled once and reached from whichever response was given (join 'any'); both missed deadlines lead to the single deemed_denied end state. no_records_released cites B.1 but also covers the B.3 not-found response.

## How do I start a business in City of Charlottesville?

**Confidence 1.00** (state law 1.00, local 1.00, contact 1.00; state model checked by Claude).

Before or alongside opening, register any LLC, corporation, or trade name (DBA) with the Virginia State Corporation Commission. Get an EIN from the IRS and, if you will make taxable sales, a Virginia retail sales tax number. Next, use the city GIS Viewer to confirm the address is inside Charlottesville city limits, because some 'Charlottesville' addresses are in Albemarle County. Apply to the Commissioner of the Revenue for a city business licence within 30 days of starting business. You can upload the application through the Commissioner's Secure Portal or mail it to PO Box 2964, Charlottesville, VA 22902. The licence costs $35 if gross receipts are under $50,000 and $50 for $50,001–$100,000. Above $100,000, you pay a tax rate based on your business classification. All city businesses must also file an itemized equipment list as of January 1. Food businesses must register for the 7% meals tax and show a Blue Ridge Health District permit or inspection. Zoning permits come from Neighborhood Development Services: a Home Occupation Permit for a home business, an Occupancy Permit for a commercial location, or a Mobile Food Unit Permit for a food truck.

### What state law requires (Va. Code Ann. § 13.1-1011)

1. **Sign and deliver articles of organization** - Organizer (founder) (permission). § 13.1-1010
   > signing and delivering articles of organization to the Commission for filing
2. **Collect the $100 filing fee** - State Corporation Commission (obligation). § 13.1-1005(1)
   > For filing any one of the following, the fee shall be $100
3. **Check articles and fees** - State Corporation Commission (obligation). § 13.1-1011(D)
   > If the Commission finds that the articles of organization comply with the requirements of law and that all required fees have been paid, it shall issue a certificate of organization.
4. **Issue certificate of organization** - State Corporation Commission (obligation). § 13.1-1004(A)
   > the Commission shall by order issue the certificate if it finds that the document complies with the provisions of this chapter and that all required fees have been paid
5. **Admit certificate to record** - State Corporation Commission (obligation). § 13.1-1004(A)
   > The Commission shall admit any such certificate to record in its office.
6. **Company files certificate of assumed name** - Limited liability company, through an authorized representative (obligation). § 59.1-69(B)
   > No person shall conduct or transact business in the Commonwealth under any assumed or fictitious name unless such person files in the office of the clerk of the Commission a certificate of assumed or fictitious name.
7. **Give commissioner evidence of the filing** - Limited liability company, through an authorized representative. § 59.1-74(B)
   > evidence of the filing has been provided to the commissioner of the revenue by the person conducting business under the assumed or fictitious name
8. **Issue local business licence** - Commissioner of the revenue (permission). § 59.1-74(B)
   > No license shall be issued by a commissioner of the revenue until a certificate of assumed or fictitious name has been made and filed

### What City of Charlottesville adds

1. **Form the entity or register a trade name (DBA) with the Virginia SCC, and get your federal and state tax numbers** - Business owner; Virginia State Corporation Commission; IRS; Virginia Tax. Register any LLC or corporation, or a trade name, at www.scc.virginia.gov. Get an Employer Identification Number at www.irs.gov and, if you will make taxable sales, a Retail Sales Tax number at www.tax.virginia.gov. ([source](https://charlottesville.gov/DocumentCenter/View/14534/New-Business-License-Checklist-PDF))

If it does not come up, contact the Commissioner of the Revenue, because the address may be in the Albemarle County part of 'Charlottesville'. ([source](https://charlottesville.gov/186/Business-License-Tax))
3. **Check zoning: get a home occupation permit for a home-based business, or an occupancy permit for a commercial location** - Office of Neighborhood Development Services (Zoning), 434-970-3182. Through NDS, get a Home Occupation Permit for a home-based business, an Occupancy Permit for a commercial location, a Homestay Permit for short-term rentals, or a Mobile Food Unit Permit for a food truck or trailer. Check the detailed home-occupation standards, such as limits on non-resident workers and clients, with NDS or the current Development Code. ([source](https://charlottesville.gov/DocumentCenter/View/14534/New-Business-License-Checklist-PDF))
4. **Apply for the city business licence within 30 days of starting business** - Commissioner of the Revenue (mail: PO Box 2964, Charlottesville, VA 22902). Complete the Business License Application. Upload it through the Commissioner's Secure Portal (choose Category 'Business License Application') or mail it to PO Box 2964, Charlottesville, VA 22902. ([source](https://charlottesville.gov/186/Business-License-Tax))
5. **Register for other local taxes and file business personal property** - Commissioner of the Revenue. File an itemized list of all equipment used in the business as of January 1. Food establishments must also submit a Meals Tax Registration form (the tax is 7%, due by the 20th of each month) and a copy of their Blue Ridge Health District health permit or inspection. Food trucks also need a Mobile Food Unit Permit from NDS. ([source](https://charlottesville.gov/186/Business-License-Tax))
6. **Get any trade, professional, health, or alcohol licences that apply** - Virginia DPOR, DHP, ABC; Blue Ridge Health District; VDACS. The Commissioner's New Business License Checklist lists these. DPOR handles professional licences and DHP handles health-professional licences. ABC handles alcohol licensing. Most food businesses need a Blue Ridge Health District health permit (434-972-6219), and bakeries need a VDACS food handling permit (804-625-3033). Building-trades contractors need proof of workers' compensation coverage, and hemp product sellers deal with the VDACS Office of Hemp Enforcement. ([source](https://charlottesville.gov/DocumentCenter/View/14534/New-Business-License-Checklist-PDF))
7. **Renew each year** - Business owner; Commissioner of the Revenue. State BPOL law requires a business licensed the year before to apply by March 1 of the licence year, though a locality may set a later date up to May 1. The city is moving to paperless renewals through its online Business Tax Portal starting with the 2026 licence cycle. You can still renew by mailing a Business License Application with payment. ([source](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title58.1/chapter37/section58.1-3703.1/))

- The renewal deadline above comes from the state BPOL statute. The city's Business License Tax page lists no renewal due date, and Virginia localities may set a later date up to May 1. Confirm with the Commissioner of the Revenue (434-970-3170).

- Only the LLC filing path is modelled step by step (articles of organization under § 13.1-1011). The other filed structures do not all follow it: a limited partnership is formed when the Commission files its certificate and admits it to record (§ 50-73.11 C, § 50-73.17 A.5), with no certificate issued; a partnership becomes an LLP when its statement of registration is filed, and the Commission must register any partnership that submits a completed statement with the fee (§ 50-73.132 A, B). Only corporations follow a certificate-issuance pattern like the LLC's (§§ 13.1-621, 13.1-820, not printed here). Their content and compliance checks are not modelled.
- State formation is only half of starting a business: most Virginia localities also require a local business licence (BPOL) under their own ordinances, authorised by Va. Code § 58.1-3700 et seq. Fee caps by population are in § 58.1-3703 A; the licence itself and its tax rates come from the local ordinance, which is scored separately.
- § 59.1-69 applies to any 'person' (§ 1-230), so it reaches entities trading under a name other than their own, not just sole proprietors. The certificate's contents (§ 59.1-70 A) are cited, not checked step by step.
- The local licence is one step (local_license), issued by the commissioner of the revenue (§ 59.1-74 B; see also § 58.1-3703 E) for the fee the locality's governing body may charge (§ 58.1-3703 A). It is reached by either of two exclusive paths that rejoin: directly after the certificate is recorded, or, for a company trading under an assumed name, only after the certificate of assumed name is filed and evidence of the filing given to the commissioner of the revenue (§ 59.1-74 B). The evidence is modelled only where the locality requires a licence, since the statute makes it a precondition of the licence and requires it nowhere else; a trade-name company in a locality without a licence ends at llc_exists after filing its certificate. The choice after recordation is not mutually exclusive: a company with an assumed name in a locality that requires a licence satisfies both uses_assumed_name and locality_requires_license. The model is correct only if the exclusive gateway evaluates its branches in the order written (uses_assumed_name first), as BPMN engines that follow document order do; an engine that picked the direct licence branch would issue the licence without the assumed-name filing that § 59.1-74 B requires first. The flow conditions take one variable per step, so the direct branch cannot be guarded by 'not uses_assumed_name' without also blocking the trade-name branch, which reaches the same step. The second locality_requires_license gateway after the evidence is always true there. On every path the LLC already exists from issuance (§ 13.1-1004 B).
- The nonstock corporation sections printed (§ 13.1-815.1, § 13.1-816) are the versions 'effective until January 1, 2027'; the replacement versions were not in the corpus and should be checked after that date.
- Nonstock status is a state corporate form, not tax exemption; federal 501(c) status is outside the Code of Virginia and not modelled.
- Professionals are not required to use a professional entity: §§ 13.1-542.1 and 13.1-1101.1 also allow ordinary corporations and LLCs unless other law or regulation prohibits it. Architects, engineers and land surveyors have extra ownership rules (§ 13.1-549, § 13.1-1111). Professional entities do not shield a professional's own professional liability (see §§ 13.1-547 and 13.1-1109, not printed here).
- A limited partner who participates in control of the business can lose the shield toward people who reasonably believe he is a general partner (§ 50-73.24 A); not modelled.
- Annual registration fees and continuation reports (e.g. § 13.1-1062 for LLCs, $50 LLP annual continuation report in § 50-73.83 F.2) are not modelled.
