## How do I request public records in City of Baltimore?

**Confidence 1.00** (state law 1.00, local 1.00, contact 1.00; state model checked by Claude).

In Baltimore City, you request public records under the Maryland Public Information Act (MPIA), Md. Code, General Provisions Title 4. There is no single citywide online portal on official city pages. Each city agency handles its own requests, so you send a written request to the agency that holds the records. Some agencies have a webform (the Board of Municipal and Zoning Appeals). Others take email or mail (Public Works, Housing & Community Development, the Inspector General, the Board of Finance). State law makes the first 2 hours of search and preparation free. After that the agency can charge a reasonable fee based on its actual costs. The fee can be waived if you cannot afford it or if waiving it is in the public interest. Agencies must respond promptly, and in no case later than 30 days. If you are denied, you must get a written statement of the reasons and your options. You can then ask the State Public Access Ombudsman for help resolving the dispute, or file a complaint in circuit court.

### What state law requires (Md. Code, General Provisions § 4-203)

1. **Submit written application to the custodian** - Applicant (person or governmental unit) (obligation). § 4-202(a)
   > shall submit a written application to the custodian
2. **Tell applicant who the custodian is** - Individual who received the application (not the custodian), within 10 business days (obligation). § 4-202(c)(1)
   > notice of that fact
3. **Notify applicant the record does not exist** - Custodian of the public record, within 30 days (obligation). § 4-202(d)
   > a custodian determines that the record does not exist, the custodian shall notify the applicant of this determination
4. **Send delay notice: time, fee range, reason** - Custodian of the public record, within 10 business days (obligation). § 4-203(b)(2)
   > If the custodian reasonably believes that it will take more than 10 working days to produce the public record, the custodian shall indicate in writing or by electronic mail
5. **Grant or deny the application** - Custodian of the public record, within 30 days (obligation). § 4-203(a)(1)
   > the custodian shall grant or deny the application promptly, but not more than 30 days after receiving the application.
6. **Send written denial: reasons, authority, record description, remedies** - Custodian of the public record, within 10 business days (obligation). § 4-203(c)(1)
   > A custodian who denies the application shall
7. **Allow inspection of any inspectable part** - Custodian of the public record (obligation). § 4-203(c)(1)(ii)
   > allow inspection of any part of the record that is subject to inspection
8. **Decide what fee to charge** - Official custodian (fees) (permission). § 4-206(b)(1)
   > the official custodian may charge an applicant a reasonable fee for

### What City of Baltimore adds

1. **Identify which city agency holds the records (each agency handles its own requests; there is no single citywide portal)** - Requester; use the agency's own MPIA page or the Maryland Manual directory of Baltimore City agencies. Find the MPIA contact on the agency's page at baltimorecity.gov. To find the right city agency, the Department of Public Works (DPW) points requesters to the Maryland Manual's online directory. ([source](https://www.baltimorecity.gov/publicworks/contact-us/maryland-public-information-act-requests))
2. **Put the request in writing, describe the records specifically, and give a way to reply** - Requester, writing to the agency's records contact (example: DPW, Dominic Lamartina). Send it by email (DPW prefers email but does not require it) or by U.S. mail. Describe the records reasonably and specifically, and include an email or return mailing address. ([source](https://www.baltimorecity.gov/publicworks/contact-us/maryland-public-information-act-requests))
3. **Use an agency webform where one exists (example: Board of Municipal and Zoning Appeals)** - Board of Municipal and Zoning Appeals (BMZA). Fill out the BMZA MPIA Request Webform. Or download the BMZA Document Request Form and email it to bmza@baltimorecity.gov with the subject line 'MPIA Request'. Or mail it to BMZA, Attn: Carolyn Young, MPIA Request, 417 E. Fayette Street, Room 922, Baltimore, MD 21202. ([source](https://www.baltimorecity.gov/zoning/how-to-view-records))
4. **Agencies that take requests by email (example: Housing & Community Development)** - Department of Housing & Community Development (DHCD), which routes MPIA requests for processing. Fill out the DHCD MPIA request form and email it to dhcd.mpia@baltimorecity.gov. If you want a search for outstanding fees and violations on a property, that is not an MPIA request. You apply for it through a separate process run by the City's Finance Department. ([source](https://www.baltimorecity.gov/dhcd/contact/public-information-act-request))
5. **Requests to the Office of the Inspector General** - Baltimore City Office of the Inspector General (OIG). Email oig@baltimorecity.gov and Dereka.Bolden@baltimorecity.gov with the subject line 'MD Public Information Act Request'. ([source](https://www.baltimorecity.gov/inspector-general/contact/public-information-act-request))
6. **Know the fee rules: the first 2 hours are free, and after that fees must be reasonable and tied to actual costs** - Agency records custodian (state law applies to all city agencies). Under GP § 4-206(c), the first 2 hours of search and preparation are free. After that, the fee must reasonably relate to the agency's actual costs. DPW provides records at no cost if the request takes under 2 hours. For longer requests it gives a written time estimate first. ([source](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=ggp&section=4-206&enactments=false))
7. **Ask for a fee waiver if you cannot pay or the request serves the public interest** - Requester, asking the custodian. Ask for a waiver. Under GP § 4-206(e), the custodian may waive fees if you are indigent and file an affidavit of indigency. It may also waive fees if, after considering your ability to pay and other factors, it finds a waiver is in the public interest. ([source](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=ggp&section=4-206&enactments=false))
8. **Expect a response within the legal deadline** - Agency records custodian. State law requires the custodian to grant or deny the request promptly, and no later than 30 days after receiving it. Within 10 days of receipt, DPW sends either the records or a written notice that processing may take up to 30 days. ([source](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=ggp&section=4-203&enactments=false))
9. **If you are denied, get a written statement of the reasons, the legal basis, and your options** - Agency records custodian. A denial must come in writing within 10 working days. It must give the reasons, the legal authority, a brief description of the record being withheld, and notice of your remedies. You must still be allowed to see any part of the record that is open to inspection (GP § 4-203(c)). ([source](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=ggp&section=4-203&enactments=false))
10. **Appeal option 1: ask the Maryland Public Access Ombudsman for help resolving the dispute** - Maryland Public Access Ombudsman (state office, 200 St. Paul Place, Baltimore, MD 21202). Use the Request Mediation link on the Ombudsman's website, or call 410-576-6560. ([source](https://piaombuds.maryland.gov/))
11. **Appeal option 2: file a complaint in circuit court** - Circuit court for the county where you live or have your principal place of business, or where the record is located (for Baltimore City, the Circuit Court for Baltimore City). After you are denied inspection, file a complaint under GP § 4-362(a). ([source](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=ggp&section=4-362&enactments=false))

### Local law

- [Md. Code, General Provisions §§ 4-101 et seq. (Maryland Public Information Act), incl. § 4-203 (time limits and denials)](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=ggp&section=4-203&enactments=false): State law that applies to all Baltimore City agencies. Custodians must grant or deny a request within 30 days. A denial must be in writing within 10 working days and give the reasons, legal authority, a description of the withheld record, and the requester's remedies. Any part of the record open to inspection must still be released.
- [Md. Code, General Provisions § 4-206 (fees)](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=ggp&section=4-206&enactments=false): A reasonable fee must reasonably relate to the agency's actual costs. The first 2 hours of search and preparation are free. Fees may be waived for indigent requesters who file an affidavit, or when a waiver is in the public interest.
- [Md. Code, General Provisions § 4-362 (judicial review)](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=ggp&section=4-362&enactments=false): A person denied inspection may file a complaint in the circuit court for the county where they live or have their principal place of business, or where the record is located.

**Fees.** There is no application fee. Under GP § 4-206(c), the first 2 hours of search and preparation are free. After that, the agency may charge a reasonable fee tied to its actual costs. DPW provides records at no cost if a request takes under 2 hours. For longer requests it gives a written estimate first, and it bases the fee on staff and attorney salaries prorated for the time spent. Fees may be waived for indigent requesters who file an affidavit, or when a waiver is in the public interest. The agency pages reviewed did not list per-page copy charges.

### Caveats and gaps

- There is no single citywide MPIA portal on official Baltimore City pages. Requests go agency by agency, and BMZA was the only agency with a webform among the pages checked.
- The city website has moved to www.baltimorecity.gov, and many older subdomain links (law.baltimorecity.gov, finance.baltimorecity.gov, publicworks.baltimorecity.gov) now redirect or no longer work. The Law Department's own PIA page was not verified. Older listings give a different room and phone (City Hall Room 171, (443) 984-3421) than the OIG page (Room 101, (410) 396-3297).
- The Baltimore Police Department reportedly handles its own MPIA requests through its Document Compliance Unit (baltimorepolice.org). This was not checked against an official page.
- The Ombudsman's home page does not say whether mediation is voluntary or binding, or which kinds of disputes it covers. Reports say that since July 1, 2022, requesters must first try the Ombudsman before complaining to the State PIA Compliance Board. This was not confirmed on an official page.
- Agency contact names and emails change. Confirm them before you send a request.
- The applicant may be a person or a governmental unit (§ 4-202(a)); the schema gives the actor one kind, so it is typed as a private party.
- No written application is needed for records on the official custodian's § 4-201(c)(2) list, or when the custodian waives the requirement (§ 4-202(b)); the model always starts from a written application.
- If the custodian decides on initial review that the record does not exist, the applicant must be told immediately (§ 4-202(d)(1)); the model uses only the 30-day outer limit for the after-search case in § 4-202(d)(2).
- Any time limit in § 4-203 may be extended by up to 30 days with the applicant's consent (§ 4-203(d)(1)); this consent exchange is not modelled as a step. Without such an extension (or a pending dispute under § 4-203(d)(2)), production stays capped at 30 days after receipt even when a delay notice was sent; the time stated in the notice does not extend it.
- Failure to produce the record in accordance with § 4-203(b) is a denial (§ 4-203(b)(3)). The model shows two separate outcomes: 'deemed denial' when the 30-day production timer passes without production (on_miss), and 'record produced late' when the record was delivered, but only after more than the reasonable period needed to retrieve it (§ 4-203(b)(1)), even if within 30 days; the second still counts as a denial although the applicant has the record. The bona-fide-dispute qualifier and the remedies for review of a denial are in other parts of Title 4 that are not modelled; they are recorded as a cross-reference. The text does not say whether such a denial triggers the written statement duty of § 4-203(c), so both outcomes end without one.
- The delay notice under § 4-203(b)(2) is due within 10 working days of receipt, while the grant/deny decision may take up to 30 days, so the model sends it (only when the custodian reasonably believes production will take more than 10 working days) before the decision; the notice branch and the no-notice path rejoin at the decision. If the notice is not sent within 10 working days, the timer also leads to the decision: the process does not end, and the duties to grant or deny and to produce within 30 days continue (§ 4-203(a)(1), (b)(1)). The only stated effect of skipping the notice is that a later failure to produce may not be considered the result of a bona fide dispute (§ 4-203(b)(3)). The notice carries only an estimate of the range of fees, so it does not wait for the § 4-206 fee decision. If the application is later denied, the text does not say what becomes of a notice already sent.
- § 4-203(c)(1)(i) requires the written denial statement within 10 working days but does not say what starts the clock; the model counts from the denial. The text gives no consequence for a late statement, and nothing in it cancels the separate duty under § 4-203(c)(1)(ii) to allow partial inspection, so a missed 10-day timer leads on to partial inspection instead of ending the process; the duty to give the statement is not lifted by the delay.
- The written denial statement must give (1) the reasons, (2) for a § 4-343 denial, why it is necessary and why redaction would not work, (3) the legal authority, (4) a brief description of the undisclosed record that lets the applicant assess the authority, without disclosing the protected information, and (5) notice of the remedies (§ 4-203(c)(1)(i)); these are contents of one notice, not separate steps.
- § 4-203(c)(1) lists the written statement (i) and partial inspection (ii) as two duties of the denying custodian without ordering them; the model puts partial inspection after the statement. Duty (ii) covers 'any part of the record that is subject to inspection', so the step is unconditional and is empty when no part is inspectable (the ruling table's 'part_inspectable' input only changes the ruling text).
- The grounds for denying inspection are in other sections of Title 4 that are not included here; the 'inspection_allowed' input of the ruling table stands in for them.
- A custodian may not ignore an application on the grounds that it was intended for harassment (§ 4-203(c)(2)); this prohibition is not a separate step.
- Fee details not modelled as table rules: fees for customized formats (§ 4-206(b)(1)(i)), proration of staff and attorney review costs (§ 4-206(b)(2)), charges for reproduction facilities the custodian lacked (§ 4-206(d)(2)), the definition of 'indigent' (§ 4-206(a)(2)), and the duty of a local school system custodian who charges a fee to give written notice that the applicant may complain to the Board (§ 4-206(f)). The fee table splits the search/preparation fee (2-hour rule, § 4-206(b)-(c)) from the copy fee (another law, § 4-206(d)(1)), because (d)(1) displaces only copy, printout and photograph fees. A waiver is discretionary ('may waive') and is decided as a separate step on top of the computed fee.
- The official custodian and the custodian may be different people; the text does not say how fee decisions are coordinated with production.
- Missing the 30-day grant/deny limit of § 4-203(a) has no stated consequence in these sections (the deemed denial of § 4-203(b)(3) is tied to production by a custodian who approves), so that timer keeps an end event of its own.

## How do I start a business in City of Baltimore?

**Confidence 1.00** (state law 1.00, local 1.00, contact 1.00; state model checked by Claude).

To start a business in Baltimore City, first register your business with the State Department of Assessments and Taxation (SDAT) through Maryland Business Express and make sure it stays in good standing. The City's business license application asks for your SDAT number. Most businesses that sell goods also need a state trader's license from the Clerk of the Circuit Court and a Maryland sales and use tax account. Baltimore City has its own trader's license fee schedule, from $20 up to $2,125 depending on the value of your stock-in-trade. Certain regulated activities need a city business license from the Department of Consumer Protection and Business Licensing (DCPBL). Examples are amusement devices, auctioneers, pawnbrokers, late-night operations, commercial parking, street vending, and massage establishments. You can apply online, by email, or by mail. Depending on your location and use, you may also need a Use & Occupancy permit through DHCD's ePermits system and an annual Fire Prevention Permit. Food businesses need Health Department plan review and a license. Home-based businesses must follow the home-occupation rules in Zoning Code § 15-507.

### What state law requires (Md. Code, Corporations and Associations § 4A-202)

1. **File articles of organization for record** - Founder (person forming the business) (permission). § 4A-202(a)

2. **Collect the $100 processing fee** - State Department of Assessments and Taxation (obligation). § 1-203(b)(4)

3. **Accept no nonconforming or unpaid articles** - State Department of Assessments and Taxation (prohibition). § 4A-207(a)(1)
   > The Department may not accept for record or filing any document of a limited liability company that does not conform with law.
4. **Endorse acceptance with date and time** - State Department of Assessments and Taxation (obligation). § 4A-207(c)(1)
   > Endorse on the document its acceptance for record and the date and time of acceptance
5. **Record the articles promptly** - State Department of Assessments and Taxation (obligation). § 4A-207(c)(2)
   > Record promptly the document
6. **Send acknowledgment of acceptance** - State Department of Assessments and Taxation (obligation). § 4A-207(c)(3)
   > Send an acknowledgment to the limited liability company, its attorney, or its agent stating the date and time that the document was accepted for record
7. **File trade name certificate before trading** - The limited liability company, its attorney, or its agent (obligation). § 1-406(a)
   > engaged in any mercantile, trading, or manufacturing business as an agent or doing business or trading under any designation, title, or name other than the person’s own name, prior to commencing operation of the business, shall file with the Department a certificate
8. **Collect $25 trade name recording fee** - State Department of Assessments and Taxation (obligation). § 1-406(e)(1)

9. **Record certificate in agency record** - State Department of Assessments and Taxation (obligation). § 1-406(d)(2)
   > The agency record shall record all certificates filed in accordance with this section, and the certificates shall be properly indexed.

### What City of Baltimore adds

1. **Register the business with the state and get an SDAT number** - Maryland State Department of Assessments and Taxation (SDAT), through Maryland Business Express. Register online at Maryland Business Express. You will need the SDAT number for the Baltimore City business license application. ([source](https://www.baltimorecity.gov/dcpbl/apply-for-a-license))
2. **Confirm your business is in good standing with Maryland** - SDAT / Maryland Business Express. Check your good standing on Maryland Business Express before you apply for city licenses. ([source](https://www.baltimorecity.gov/dcpbl/apply-for-a-license))
3. **Open a Maryland sales and use tax account and get a trader's license if you sell goods** - Comptroller of Maryland (sales tax); Clerk of the Circuit Court for Baltimore City (trader's license). Register for sales and use tax with the Comptroller, then apply for a trader's license from the Clerk of the Circuit Court. Growers and manufacturers are exempt. The license year runs May 1 to April 30. ([source](https://www.marylandcomptroller.gov/businesses/new-business/business-licenses.html))
4. **Check the trader's license fee (based on the value of your stock-in-trade; Baltimore City has its own schedule)** - Clerk of the Circuit Court for Baltimore City. Under Business Regulation § 17-1807(c)(3), Baltimore City fees range from $20 (stock-in-trade of $1,000 or less) to $2,125 (more than $750,000). Most other counties start at $15. ([source](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=gbr&section=17-1807&enactments=false))
5. **Apply for a Baltimore City business license if your activity is regulated** - Department of Consumer Protection and Business Licensing (DCPBL), Business Licensing Division. Pay the application fee online at pay.baltimorecity.gov. Submit the application and any required addendum online (Jotform), by email to COBBusinessLicense@baltimorecity.gov, or by mail to 200 Holliday Street, RM 3, Baltimore, MD 21202. Regulated activities include amusement devices, auctioneers, pawnbrokers, street entertainers, late-night commercial operations, commercial and special-event parking, street, mobile and merchant vendors, and massage establishments. ([source](https://www.baltimorecity.gov/dcpbl/apply-for-a-license))
6. **Get a Use & Occupancy permit for a new tenant, change of use, or change of ownership** - Department of Housing & Community Development (DHCD), through ePermits. Apply in the ePermits system under the 'Use and occupancy permit' category. A final inspection is required before the permit is issued. ([source](https://www.baltimorecity.gov/building-permits/permit-information-and-requirements/permit-information-use-occupancy))
7. **Get an annual Fire Prevention Permit if your license type or occupancy requires one** - Baltimore City Fire Department, Fire Prevention Bureau. Buildings where the public works, plays or spends time are inspected every year. Check whether your license type needs a Fire Prevention Permit, and get it before you apply for the business license. ([source](https://www.baltimorecity.gov/fire/about/who-we-are/fire-prevention))
8. **Food businesses: complete Health Department plan review and get a food facility license** - Baltimore City Health Department, Environmental Health (Plan Review), 1001 E. Fayette Street; 410-396-4544. New food service facilities, and facilities under new ownership, must submit plans and pass a plan review inspection before the license is approved. ([source](https://www.baltimorecity.gov/health/environmental-health/plan-review))
9. **Home-based businesses: follow the Zoning Code home-occupation limits** - Business owner (zoning enforcement). Under § 15-507, the business must be run entirely inside the dwelling and stay secondary to living there. You may employ no more than 1 person who is not a family member living there. Client visits are limited to 3 a day and 10 a week. Outside storage and motor-vehicle repair are not allowed. ([source](https://codes.baltimorecity.gov/us/md/cities/baltimore/code/32/15-507))

Use & Occupancy, Fire Prevention, and Health permit fees vary and were not verified. SDAT formation fees were not verified on the pages reviewed.

### Caveats and gaps

- The original research said DCPBL was created in 2025 and that the city license does not accept trade-name ('T') numbers. Neither claim appears on the DCPBL pages reviewed, so both were removed.
- The license-type table seen on the DCPBL page covered addenda A–F. Other regulated categories from the original research (employment agencies, towing, second-hand dealers) were not confirmed and may be on parts of the page that were not retrieved.
- The Comptroller's business licenses page does not list trader's license fees. The figures come directly from Bus. Reg. § 17-1807.
- The original business research arrived cut off after the trader's license fee step. The later steps (licensing, U&O, fire, health, home occupation) were rebuilt and checked against official pages.
- The Clerk of the Circuit Court for Baltimore City's trader's license page and contact details were not fetched. Confirm the filing location and hours with the Clerk.
- Forming the entity (LLC, corporation, etc.) through SDAT has its own state filing fees and steps, which were not verified here.
- The filing path models the LLC; corporations follow the parallel procedure in §§ 1-201 and 1-202 (not printed here), and limited partnerships are formed, and partnerships qualify as LLPs, at filing of their certificates (§ 10-201(b), § 9A-1001(b)).
- Maryland does not issue a 'certificate of organization': the Department endorses its acceptance, records the articles and sends an acknowledgment (§ 4A-207(c)); the LLC exists from acceptance or a later time stated in the articles, if there has been substantial compliance (§ 4A-202(b)).
- Formation happens at the acceptance the Department endorses (endorse_acceptance) or a later time in the articles; the single 'LLC formed' end event sits after the acknowledgment (and after the trade name certificate is recorded, when one is needed) only because a BPMN end event cannot mark a midpoint without an unsound parallel split, and the acknowledgment states the acceptance date and time (§ 4A-207(c)(3)). The optional trade name branch rejoins the main path through an exclusive merge in front of that end event, so a company that trades under another name is still shown as formed.
- § 4A-207(a) and (b) are prohibitions (the Department may not accept nonconforming or unpaid documents); the review that applies them is implied, not stated. Under § 4A-207(a)(2) the Department may treat a document that purports to be acknowledged as properly acknowledged.
- The naming rules of Title 1, Subtitle 5 that § 4A-208 applies to the articles are not printed here; the 'name_complies' input stands for them.
- The stock-corporation organization and capitalization fee under § 1-204(c)(1) depends on the aggregate par value of the capital stock, so the stock and professional corporation fee cells say 'See 1-204'.
- The limited partnership rule fires before the LLC rule (the comparison table's fixed priority) whenever protection and passive co-owners are both wanted, although its general partner stays exposed (§ 10-403); the output points owners who all need protection to the LLC.
- § 5-102(a)(2) says a corporation eligible to be a professional corporation may not organize under any other corporate form (with the § 5-102(a)(3) exceptions); whether that bars choosing an LLC instead is not settled by the printed text, which speaks only of 'corporate form'.
- Maryland has no separate professional limited liability company statute; § 4A-203(10) lets any LLC render professional services, subject to the licensing board for the profession.
- Trader's licences under Business Regulation Title 17, Subtitle 18 are applied for with, and issued by, 'the clerk' (§ 17-1804, § 17-1805; not printed in full here); the printed sections do not name which clerk. Fees vary by county, with separate schedules for Baltimore City and Baltimore County (§ 17-1807). Growers, makers and manufacturers are exempt (§ 17-1803(d)). Local zoning, health and occupancy permits are outside the state statutes and attach in the local layer.
- The trade name certificate must be filed before the business starts operating (§ 1-406(a)); the schema has no 'before' deadline, so this stays in the step text. It must be in writing, affirmed or acknowledged under oath, and give the owner's names and addresses, the character and location of the business, the trade name, and the identification number the Department assigned to the business (§ 1-406(a)(1)-(3)), which is why it follows formation.
- The trade name certificate is effective for 5 years; the filer may renew it by applying within 6 months before the 5-year period ends, and each renewal extends it 5 years (fee $25 under § 1-406(e)(2), § 1-406(f)).
- The version of § 1-203 that takes effect October 1, 2026 (Chapters 198 and 199 of 2025) is now in force; it keeps every fee used here and adds the limited worker cooperative association (an LLC that elects that status under § 4A-12A-04, $100 articles of organization). The comparison table has no input that separates a worker cooperative from an ordinary LLC, so it is noted on the LLC row rather than given its own rule.
- State tax registration (Comptroller), employer registration and the annual report ($300, § 1-203(b)(3)(ii)) follow formation and are not modelled.
- Once the LLC is formed it is the person doing business, so the company files the trade name certificate (§ 1-406(a)), which must give the Department's identification number for the business (§ 1-406(a)(3)(iv)). § 1-406(a) also requires the certificate from anyone engaged in a mercantile, trading, or manufacturing business as an agent, even under its own name.
